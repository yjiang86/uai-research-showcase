## Project brief

UAI is an independent research program asking how experience should change an AI system’s persistent state across multiple timescales. I lead the research framing, mechanism specification, evaluation criteria, and AI-assisted engineering.

The implemented prototypes use versioned Engram objects with admission checks, provenance, conflict handling, invalidation, rollback, replay, and consolidation. Their purpose is to govern what can be written, expressed, and promoted, extending beyond retrieval alone. Controlled synthetic studies isolated lifecycle-policy effects under oracle labels, while public-data comparisons exposed weaknesses in the standalone retrieval path. I used these results, including the withdrawal of confounded positive interpretations, to refine the next experimental design rather than preserve an unsupported headline.

A second line tests partial runtime-to-training internalization through gate-conditioned learning and small recurrent circuit models. Approximately parameter-matched recurrent and GRU controls did not establish structural superiority; within-model closure studies instead identified conditional gains under an explicit governance-cost model.

The supporting software includes source-linked literature and claim registries, plus a provenance-aware scientific-planning interface that separates evidence, hypotheses, and durable knowledge updates. Together, these deliverables demonstrate hypothesis-driven research design, executable systems, and controlled evaluation. They remain bounded prototypes and mechanism studies, with large-model continual learning, biological equivalence, and autonomous scientific discovery left as unproven goals.
